4. For iPhone photos (desktop app)

Turning a HEIC photo into a JPEG or PNG uses heic-convert and heic-decode (ISC), jpeg-js (BSD 3-Clause), pngjs (MIT) and libheif-js, a build of struktur AG's libheif and libde265.

libheif-js is under the GNU Lesser General Public License 3.0. ShareSecure uses it unchanged, as a separate file (node_modules/libheif-js inside the app's resources), so you can replace it with your own build. Its source code is at the links above; if you can't get it there, email us and we'll send it.

5. FFmpeg, downloaded when you convert

Converting videos, audio and some images in the desktop app uses FFmpeg. It isn't part of ShareSecure and isn't in its installer: the first time you convert something that needs it, the app asks, then downloads the build published by the ffmpeg-static project (release b6.1.1) from GitHub, checks it against a fingerprint fixed in ShareSecure's code, and keeps it on your computer. It runs as a separate program, and only ever on your own files.

Those builds (by gyan.dev for Windows, evermeet.cx and osxexperts.net for macOS, and John Van Sickle for Linux) are licensed under the GNU General Public License 3.0. Their license, and notes on how each was built and where its source code is, are saved next to it as ffmpeg.LICENSE and ffmpeg.README, in the ffmpeg folder of ShareSecure's app data (%APPDATA%\ShareSecure on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/ShareSecure on a Mac, ~/.config/ShareSecure on Linux). FFmpeg's source code is at ffmpeg.org.

Some formats FFmpeg and libheif handle, such as H.264, HEVC and AAC, may be covered by patents in some countries. ShareSecure gives no patent license for them. Converting is always optional.
