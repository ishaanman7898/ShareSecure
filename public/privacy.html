Privacy policy

Last updated October 28, 2026 · Every change, on GitHub

The short version

  • We ask for a username and password. No email, no phone number, no real name.
  • Files are end-to-end encrypted by default: we store them, but we can't read them, their names or their notes.
  • Shares go up with anonymous tokens, so we can't tell which account shared what.
  • No analytics, no ads, no tracking cookies. Our code doesn't log IP addresses.
  • Everything is erased when it expires, and you can delete your account and files at any time.
  • ShareSecure is free. There's nothing to pay for, so we never ask for payment details.

This policy covers the ShareSecure website at sharesecure-du8.pages.dev. A copy of ShareSecure you run yourself (the desktop app, or a self-hosted server) keeps your data on your own machine; section 9 covers what still reaches the internet. ShareSecure's code is open source, so you can check everything here against what it actually does.

1. What we store

Your account

Your username, what's needed to check your password without ever having it (never the password itself), and when you signed up. Your public key, and your private key locked with a key only your browser has. If you connect an AI assistant, a hash of its token and your rule for what it may send.

The public key log

Your public key is added to a public, append-only log, so nobody (us included) can quietly swap it. Entries name an account by a hash of its username, but someone who guesses a username can hash it and see that the account exists. The log can't be edited, so an entry stays after you delete your account; the log then records the deletion.

Files you share

The file, its name and its type, encrypted. Alongside them: the file's size (padded to a standard size for end-to-end files), when it was uploaded and expires, how many times it's been opened, and the settings you chose (view only, works once, passcode needed, and so on). Notes and drawings are encrypted too. Links reshared from yours point at the same encrypted file; which link a reshare came from is stored only as a keyed code, so the database alone can't show who shared with whom.

If you turn on a watermark or “discourage screenshots” for a share, that setting is sealed inside the file's encrypted name, so we can't see or change it. The watermark itself is drawn in the viewer's browser; we never learn who looked at a file.

Who uploaded what

Everything shared from the website, the desktop app or the assistant on your own computer is end-to-end encrypted and goes up with anonymous tokens, never your sign-in, so nothing ties it to your account. Deleting a share uses its delete key, not your sign-in, so that doesn't tie it to you either. Files shared by an assistant connected through our servers are tagged with a code derived from your account with a secret key, not your username or account number. Your list of shares (“Your shares”) is kept on our servers sealed to your own key, so it follows you between devices and we can't read it.

Daily limits

To enforce daily limits, we count how many anonymous tokens each account picks up a day (under a keyed code) and the files tagged to an account in the last 24 hours. Spent tokens are remembered for two days so none can be used twice.

Files sent to you, and file requests

When someone sends you a file, we store it with its encrypted note and whether you've accepted it. Sends with an anonymous token can't be traced to the sender. For sends made while signed in, a keyed code standing in for the sender is kept on the copy until you accept it, and in a log erased after 24 hours. File requests you make are stored under your account with what you asked for sealed so we can't read it; files sent through them are sealed to your key.

Assistants, sends waiting for approval, and links that work once

When an assistant sends a file to someone not on your list, the send waits sealed to your own key for up to a week; your list of people is sealed the same way. After a Works once link is opened, we keep its id, a hash of its delete key and how many times someone tried it again, for 30 days, so we can tell you about those tries.

2. What we don't collect

No email address, phone number or real name. No analytics, advertising or tracking scripts, and no cookies. Our own code doesn't record IP addresses, browser details or who viewed a file. We don't sell, rent or share your data, and we never use files, or anything else on ShareSecure, to train AI.

3. How we use it

Only to run ShareSecure: to store and serve your files until they expire, deliver what you send, enforce the limits, keep accounts secure. We don't build profiles, and we don't look through files (we can't open end-to-end encrypted ones). We may act on a specific file or account if it's reported as breaking the terms or the law requires it.

5. How long we keep it

  • A file's link stops working the moment it expires (after 1 hour to 10 days, as you choose), and the file and everything stored about it are erased at the next cleanup, which runs whenever anyone uploads.
  • Deleting a file, or declining one sent to you, erases it straight away. A Works once file is erased when it's opened.
  • File requests go when they expire (30 days at most) or you close them.
  • Your account stays until you delete it (Settings → Delete account), which erases it and every file shared from it right away. The key log keeps a record that the account existed (section 1).

6. What stays in your browser

Your sign-in lasts until you close the browser. While you're signed in, your private key is kept in the browser in a form it can use but never read out, and it's deleted when you sign out. The browser also keeps: your list of shares and their delete keys (up to 30 days), a few anonymous tokens, the public keys of people you've sent to, the last state of the key log it checked, that you accepted the terms, and your Settings (notifications, the crosshair, what new shares start with). None of it leaves your device, and you can clear it in your browser's settings. If you install ShareSecure to your phone's home screen, things you share to it wait on the phone until you choose to share them.

7. How your files are protected

Your browser (or the desktop app) encrypts the file, its name, any note and any drawings (AES-256-GCM) before they're uploaded, and the key lives only in the link after the #, which browsers never send to a server. Files sent to a username have their key sealed to that person's account. Your password never reaches us: signing in proves you know it without sending it.

Files shared by an assistant connected through our servers, and files from older versions that weren't end-to-end encrypted, are encrypted on the server with a key derived from a master key we hold, so whoever controls the server could read them while they exist. If that matters, use the assistant that runs on your computer, or run ShareSecure yourself.

Watermarks and screenshot deterrents make leaks traceable and casual copying harder; they can't stop screenshots or a phone camera, and we don't claim they do. See the security page for the details and how to report a problem. If we ever learn of a breach affecting your data, we'll say so on the website and in the changelog as soon as we can.

  • Turn things off. Disconnect AI assistants, turn off notifications and change what new shares start with in Settings.

9. The desktop app and self-hosted copies

When you run ShareSecure yourself, your account, files and encryption key stay on your own computer. We never receive any of it unless you link a ShareSecure account. A few things still reach the internet:

  • The desktop app makes or links a ShareSecure account with your username when you set it up or sign in, unless you link another or unlink it. Each file you send to a username is uploaded as an encrypted copy under that account, and this policy covers it. Files sent to your username are fetched from the website and kept on your computer once you accept them; the account's private key is kept on your computer, encrypted, to open them.
  • To make links work from anywhere, the app keeps a connection open to ShareSecure's relay (a Cloudflare Worker), and people opening a link reach your computer through it, so they never learn its address. Shares are locked end to end before they leave the app, so the relay only carries locked bytes it can't read, and it keeps no log, addresses or files. The relay does see your computer's address while it's connected, and the addresses of people opening links, as any website does. You can turn it off with the USE_RELAY setting if you have your own domain.
  • The app checks GitHub for updates.

The desktop app can hide windows showing view-only files from screenshots and screen recordings (Windows and macOS). That setting stays on your computer.

10. Children

ShareSecure isn't meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly let them use it. If you think a child has an account, email us and we'll delete it.

11. Questions people ask

Can ShareSecure read my files?

Not end-to-end encrypted ones, which is the default. The key never reaches us. We can read files you upload with end-to-end encryption turned off, while they exist.

Does the person I send to learn who I am?

A link doesn't say who made it. A file sent to a username arrives without the sender's name unless you write it in the note.

Can someone screenshot my file?

Yes, if they're determined. No website can fully stop it. The watermark makes a screenshot traceable to one opening of the link, and the desktop app can hide files from screen capture.

Do you sell data or show ads?

No. ShareSecure is free and run by one person; there are no ads and nothing is sold.

Where can I see how this policy has changed?

Every version of this page is in the project's history on GitHub.

12. Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update this page and the date at the top, note it in the changelog, and the change will be visible line by line on GitHub. We won't use data we already hold in a new way without saying so here first.
